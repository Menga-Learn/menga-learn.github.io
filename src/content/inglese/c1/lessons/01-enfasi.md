## Enfasi: inversione, frasi scisse e participiali

Al C1 la grammatica serve a **dare rilievo** alle idee e a scrivere in modo **compatto**. Le strutture di questa lezione distinguono un testo "corretto" da uno "efficace".

### Inversione dopo avverbi negativi o restrittivi

Quando l'avverbio va a inizio frase, l'ordine diventa **ausiliare + soggetto**, come in una domanda:

- ***Never have I seen** such a chaotic meeting.*
- ***Rarely does** the board **agree** so quickly.*
- ***Not until** the results were published **did we realise** the scale of the problem.*
- ***Only after** the merger **did** the problems **become** clear.*
- ***No sooner had** we arrived **than** it started to rain.* / ***Hardly had** we sat down **when** the alarm went off.*
- ***Under no circumstances should** you share your password.*

Anche nei condizionali formali, senza *if*: ***Had I known**, I would have helped* (= If I had known). ***Should you need** further information, please contact us.*

### Frasi scisse (cleft sentences)

Spostano l'attenzione su un elemento:

- ***It was** the marketing team **that** suggested the change* (non un altro team).
- ***What** I find surprising **is** the lack of data.*
- ***All** we need **is** a clear deadline.*
- ***The reason why** they left **was that** they felt undervalued.*

### Frasi participiali

Rendono il testo più compatto e formale:

- ***Having finished** the report, she sent it to the client.* (= After she had finished…)
- ***Faced with** rising costs, many firms cut staff.* (= Because they were faced with…)
- *The figures **published** yesterday show a decline.* (= which were published)

Attenzione al **soggetto sospeso**: ~~Walking into the room, the lights went off~~ (le luci non camminano!) → *As I walked into the room, the lights went off.*

### Nominalizzazione

Lo stile accademico e professionale trasforma i verbi in nomi:

*The company **expanded** rapidly, which **worried** investors* → *The company's **rapid expansion** caused investor **concern**.*
