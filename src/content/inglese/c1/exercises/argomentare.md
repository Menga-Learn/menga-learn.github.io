## Argomentare e rispondere alle obiezioni

Parla per **2–3 minuti** sul tema:

> *Some people argue that cities should ban private cars from their historic centres. What is your view?*

Poi rispondi in **1 minuto** a questa obiezione:

> *But wouldn't that make life very difficult for elderly and disabled residents?*

Registrati e trascrivi l'introduzione e la risposta all'obiezione.

<!-- SOLUZIONE -->

### Risposta modello (estratti)

**Inquadrare e posizionarsi**
*This is a more complex issue than it might first appear, because it's not so much about cars as about what we want city centres to be for. On balance, I'd come down in favour of a ban, provided it's introduced gradually and with proper alternatives.*

**Sviluppare**
*Take Pontevedra in Spain, for instance, where most traffic was removed from the centre. The knock-on effects were striking: local shops actually benefited from increased footfall, and air quality improved noticeably…*

**Concessione**
*Of course, one could argue that this simply pushes traffic into the surrounding neighbourhoods. That's a valid concern, and it's why any ban would need to go hand in hand with better public transport.*

**Risposta all'obiezione**
*That's a fair point, and I think it's the strongest argument against a blanket ban. However, I'd say it highlights the need for exemptions rather than undermining the idea altogether. Residents with mobility problems could be issued permits, and many cities already run on-demand electric shuttles. So, to put it another way, the goal isn't to make the centre inaccessible, but to make it accessible to people rather than to cars.*

### Checklist

- Hai **inquadrato** la questione prima di dare la tua opinione?
- C'è almeno un **esempio concreto** e una **concessione** (*That said…, Of course one could argue…*)?
- Nella risposta all'obiezione hai **riconosciuto** il punto prima di ribatterlo?
- Hai usato strutture C1 (*It's not so much… as…*, *provided*, *rather than*)?
