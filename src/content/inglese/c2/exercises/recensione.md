## Recensione critica

Scrivi una **recensione di 250–300 parole** per una rivista culturale online su un libro, un film, una serie o una mostra che conosci bene (o inventata). La recensione deve:

1. aprire con un giudizio d'insieme incisivo;
2. dare il contesto essenziale senza riassumere la trama;
3. analizzare almeno un punto di forza e uno di debolezza con esempi;
4. chiudere con una valutazione e dire a quale pubblico è adatto.

### Una recensione modello (279 parole)

> **Quietly Devastating: *The Long Field***
>
> There are films that announce their importance from the first frame, and there are films that earn it slowly, almost reluctantly. *The Long Field* belongs firmly to the second category — and is all the better for it.
>
> Set over a single summer on a struggling farm in Northumberland, the film follows a family forced to decide whether to sell the land they have worked for four generations. It is, on the face of it, a familiar premise. What lifts it above the countless rural dramas it superficially resembles is its refusal to sentimentalise either the land or the people who cling to it.
>
> Much of the credit belongs to Ruth Abbott, whose performance as the widowed mother is a masterclass in restraint. In the film's pivotal scene, she reads the buyer's offer aloud, pauses, and simply folds the letter in half. Nothing is said; everything is understood.
>
> Not all of the film's choices are as assured. The subplot involving the younger son's ambitions in London feels underwritten, and its resolution — a single, conveniently timed phone call — rings false in a film otherwise so attentive to the texture of real life. The pacing, too, will test the patience of viewers accustomed to more conventional storytelling.
>
> These reservations aside, *The Long Field* is a rare achievement: a film about loss that never wallows, and about tradition that never preaches. Those in search of spectacle should look elsewhere. Anyone willing to meet it on its own unhurried terms, however, will find it lingers long after the credits have rolled.

### Cosa notare

- **Apertura** con un contrasto che posiziona subito il giudizio.
- Contesto in **due frasi**, poi analisi.
- Esempio preciso (la lettera piegata) invece di aggettivi generici.
- Critica concreta (*underwritten*, *rings false*) e **registro uniforme**.
- Chiusura che **indica il pubblico** con una frase bilanciata (*Those in search of… Anyone willing to…*).
